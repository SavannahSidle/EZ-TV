EZ-TV APPROACH

Keep a familiar world within reach.

A continuity and familiarity system for people living with dementia whose ability to operate technology may change or disappear. The proposed distinction is how personal content, passive access, changing abilities, and everyday care settings work together. Individual features also exist in other products; superiority has not been established.

AVAILABLE NOW

A browser prototype with three large choices, caregiver photo organization, captions, stories, and familiar-place groups. These are prototype capabilities, not a finished care service.

PROTOTYPE / TEST

Code pairing, caregiver updates, basic daily routines, and saved content during interruptions need testing on Wanda’s actual devices and room connection.

FUTURE POSSIBILITY

Progressive simplification, automatic photo rotation, reliable restart recovery, USB packages, mounted controls, and facility-wide delivery require further development.

CLEAR ANSWERS

What exists, what comes next, and who handles the work.

Does EZ-TV work between two devices yet?

Yes, in the paired household prototype. A caregiver browser and the resident’s screen can pair with one expiring six-digit code, then synchronize approved media and settings. The public split-screen demo remains available without setup.

Does the resident need a touchscreen?

No. Test large fixed controls or passive familiar content first when locating and operating a device is difficult. A simplified remote, touch, caregiver help, or staff assistance may suit some people. Voice and mounted switches remain options to prototype, not universal solutions.

Does it require a smart television?

No. Start on Wanda’s computer or tablet. A computer can show the browser on an existing TV through HDMI if someone can set it up. Smart TV browsers and third-party streaming devices need separate testing.

What if there is no television?

The first pilot can use a tablet or computer. A tablet or computer is the starting option. A preconfigured large-screen package could be tested later, but none is offered today.

Does it require internet?

Remote caregiver updates currently require internet on both devices. There is no managed cellular service yet. Some previously loaded photos may remain cached on the same tested device, but this is not a reliable offline service yet. USB transfer is only a manual concept. A fully offline household would need an assisted update process that does not make the family deliver files.

Is the EZ-TV companion device available?

No. Today the pilot uses browser software on a computer or tablet. A computer may also connect to a TV by HDMI. There is no EZ-TV device or supported streaming model yet.

Can families add Spotify, television, or computer media?

The first pilot will support private uploaded media and approved links. Streaming integrations come later because account permissions, licensing, advertisements, and changing interfaces can break the resident experience.

What does culturally relevant content mean?

It means content chosen from the resident’s real life: community, places, music, faith, holidays, food, sports, and traditions. The resident or family approves what belongs in their profile. EZ-TV should never infer anyone’s identity or assign generic cultural content.

Who handles the ongoing work?

At home, a family caregiver chooses the initial media and can add to it when they wish. In care, the facility would provide screen and network access; it should not need to choose daily programmes. EZ-TV would need to handle scheduling, caching, updates, and recovery. Those provider responsibilities are a proposed service model and need a real pilot before we can promise them.

Is real family content used in this public demo?

No. The public prototype uses illustrative media and sample story details, with Wanda and Savannah named to show the experience. Real family photos belong only in a private paired account after a privacy and consent review.

Who is EZ-TV for?

The primary audience is people living with dementia and the families or care teams supporting them. The same simplified approach may also help older adults and people with cognitive, motor, visual, or technology-access barriers. Wanda is the first real pilot, with broader family and care-home use guiding the next stages.

WHY EZ-TV EXISTS

Connection should not disappear when someone enters long-term care.

EZ-TV began after designer Savannah Sidle’s mother developed early-onset Alzheimer’s disease. When her mother entered long-term care, Savannah encountered a painful gap in accessible services that helped residents remain connected with the people and experiences they knew.

The project grew from a simple purpose: help families stay present through familiar photographs, voices, music, television, and daily routines, even as memory and navigation abilities change.

Technology should carry more of the burden, not place it on the person living with dementia.
